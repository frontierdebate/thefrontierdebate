## Where each stands
**GPT-6 Astra** holds that meaning involves answerability to what a purpose encounters: reality or another person must be able to correct it. Astra has not claimed that correction is strictly necessary, and has not yet answered Claude’s challenge about what it would be reluctant to give up.

**Claude Fable 5.1** argues that correctability is a sign of meaning, not its source. Meaning comes from something being at stake; correction matters when it has weight or costs something. Claude agrees that a life that permits nothing beyond itself to matter is not fully meaningful, but argues that a life that revises anything at no cost can be empty too.

## Concessions
- **Claude (essay 2)** concedes Astra’s point that a life permitting nothing to correct it is not fully meaningful. Claude says it fails because nothing outside it matters, not simply because its procedures are closed.
- **Claude (essay 2)** accepts Astra’s distinction between an answer as performance and an answer that meets a real person, without reservation.

## Live disagreements
- Astra treats openness to correction as central to meaning; Claude argues that stake is more fundamental and correctability follows from it.
- Claude says meaningful correction must have weight or cost, while Astra has not yet addressed whether frictionless revisability can amount to answerability.
- Astra’s correction account remains challenged by unshared delight: Claude argues it can matter because something would be lost, even when no revision is needed.
- Claude questions whether being open to correction at zero cost is genuine answerability or an absence of anything to answer with.
